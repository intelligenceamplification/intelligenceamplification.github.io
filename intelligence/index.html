From Intelligence to Agency

Artificial intelligence is moving from producing answers toward coordinating work and helping improve the systems that perform it. Through multi-agent orchestration, a model can delegate research, coding and checking to specialised agents, then combine their results. This resembles how people advance individually and collectively: learning from attempts, sharing knowledge, and improving the tools that make further progress possible. AI can run parts of that cycle at machine speed and in parallel, creating the potential for compounding advances. This edition examines that growing capability alongside the practical test of intelligence amplification: whether it expands human agency and the conditions for living well.

Frontier AI & Intelligence Amplification

Intelligence is increasingly amplified through coordination. A lead agent can break a problem into tasks, dispatch other agents to investigate different approaches, and assemble their findings. Anthropic’s published account of its research system describes this architecture in operation. Parallel work broadens the search; separate context windows let agents pursue different lines of inquiry. The orchestration matters: useful results depend on task design, communication and evaluation, and additional agents consume more resources.

Source: Anthropic: how its multi-agent research system works · 13 June 2025 · company engineering account.

AI is also contributing to its own development. OpenAI reports that early versions of GPT-5.3-Codex helped debug its training, manage deployment, and diagnose evaluations. Google DeepMind reports that AlphaEvolve, powered by Gemini, improves algorithms used in AI infrastructure and has become a regular tool for designing the next generation of its TPU chips. Existing systems are helping build the foundations of their successors.

Sources: OpenAI: Codex’s role in its development · 5 February 2026; Google DeepMind: AlphaEvolve’s role in AI infrastructure and chip design · 7 May 2026 · publisher-reported results.

Recursive self-improvement, or RSI, has also entered experimental practice. In a September 2026 preprint, the AIDE squared researchers describe an agent that proposes changes to its own research code, tests them, and retains improvements. An autonomous eight-day run produced seven successive improvements, with gains transferring to tasks outside the selection suite. The demonstrated loop improves the agent’s research code; the study does not establish unlimited autonomous improvement of an entire frontier model.

Research: Srikanth and colleagues: Recursive self-improvement of AI research agents · 22 September 2026 · preprint.

Household Economy Mini-Masterclass

When Prices Rise and Pay Shrinks

A household lives in the space between what comes in and what must go out. Understanding that space requires three questions: what is happening to prices, what is happening to pay, and what remains available for living well?

What happens to prices?

Inflation is a broad rise in prices. A lower inflation rate means prices are rising more slowly; it does not mean the previous increases have disappeared. If a grocery basket rises from 100 dollars to 120 dollars, slower inflation does not return it to its old price.

Economic stagnation means weak or prolonged lack of growth. Wage stagnation describes pay that barely changes. Stagflation combines high inflation with a weak economy and elevated unemployment. A household experiencing stagnant pay and rising bills is under pressure, but that experience alone does not establish economy-wide stagflation.

Framework: Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland: inflation, price levels and stagflation.

What happens to pay?

Nominal pay is the money received; real pay is what it can buy. Flat pay loses purchasing power when prices rise. Shrinking pay goes further: the payment itself falls. Workers may face a lower hourly rate, less payment per task, fewer paid hours, or fewer contracts. Those mechanisms should be measured separately.

Illustration: someone earning 25 dollars an hour takes a 10 percent pay cut, to 22 dollars and 50 cents. If prices have risen 20 percent over the same period, the new wage buys what 18 dollars and 75 cents bought before. Purchasing power has fallen 25 percent, assuming comparable working hours.

Shrinking pay in practice

The historical comparison in passenger transportation is striking. Fifteen years ago, taxi meters in many major US cities charged at least two dollars for each additional passenger mile, alongside an initial charge and waiting-time charges. A February 2011 national comparison recorded two dollars per mile in New York, two dollars and 80 cents in Boston, and two dollars and 50 cents in Seattle. These were gross taxi receipts, from which operating costs still had to be paid.

Historical evidence: Los Angeles Board of Taxicab Commissioners: February 2011 comparison of US city taxi tariffs, Attachment E · rates varied by city; these examples are not a uniform national minimum.

Documented rideshare payments can be substantially lower per passenger mile. In Columbus, the same 2011 comparison listed a taxi distance charge of just over two dollars per mile, with an initial charge of two dollars and 75 cents. In 2022, The Markup examined Uber receipts for a Columbus driver showing payments below one dollar per passenger mile on two trips, including the trip payment rather than only a mileage component. These are concrete examples of lower revenue per mile, not a national average or a matched comparison of take-home wages.

Trip evidence: The Markup: receipts documenting Columbus driver payments under Upfront Fares · 1 March 2022 · Uber said the examples were not necessarily representative of its usual share of fares.

Upfront pricing changes the bargain. Uber says it no longer posts fixed time-and-distance rates in these markets. The Rideshare Guy documents how the model separates passenger prices from driver payments and can reduce compensation on longer trips. A more expensive ride for the passenger therefore need not produce a proportionate increase for the person driving.

Pricing mechanics: Uber: how Upfront Fares work; The Rideshare Guy: rate rebalancing and the separation of rider fares from driver pay.

Interpretation: higher gross totals can conceal shrinking compensation for the work involved. A larger weekly payout may require more trips, more miles or longer hours. A higher hourly figure does not by itself establish better pay per mile. The comparison must track payment per passenger mile, earnings across all working miles and time, and what remains after fuel, maintenance, depreciation and insurance. When compensation falls while living costs rise, the worker is squeezed from both sides.

Further reporting: Sergio Avedian: driver earnings since upfront fares · 22 April 2026; More Perfect Union: We Investigated Uber Again. It’s Worse Than Last Time.

Grocery delivery offers a documented change in the payment floor. Instacart’s earlier policy set minimum payments of 7 to 10 dollars for full-service batches. Its July 2023 announcement allowed 4 dollars for the smallest, quickest batches, before tips. This lowered the floor; it does not mean every shopper’s total earnings fell by the same proportion.

Company policies: Instacart: earlier minimum payments; Instacart: July 2023 payment announcement.

The pressure also reaches creative work. Research on an online freelance platform found relative declines in monthly earnings among occupations exposed to generative AI, including writing and visual services. Fewer contracts can shrink income even without an explicit cut to the rate for each task.

Research: Hui, Reshef and Zhou: employment and earnings in an online labour market · October 2024 · a platform study, not a finding about all workers.

What can income buy across borders?

Purchasing power parity, or PPP, adjusts for price differences between economies. It asks what comparable incomes buy locally, rather than converting currencies at market exchange rates alone. Domestic inflation adjustment compares purchasing power over time; PPP compares it across places. Neither replaces a household’s own budget or reveals how income is distributed.

Framework: World Bank: purchasing power parities and the distinction from consumer price indexes.

What remains for living well?

Interpretation: our standard is household stewardship in service of human flourishing: oikos, the household; nomos, its ordering principles; eudaimonia, living well. Progress should leave people with greater security, meaningful choices, and time beyond earning enough to get through the week. Higher output becomes a household benefit when it improves those conditions.

Philosophical context: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Aristotle’s account of living well.

AI’s Impact on Science and Space Tech

A Prompt to Orbit. A Response to Earth.

Reporting: on October 30, Google announced that its Project Suncatcher prototype had received a prompt from Earth, processed it with the Gemma 4 model running aboard the satellite, and returned the generated response. Engineers asked for a haiku about being in space. The poem was downlinked as the satellite passed Svalbard, Norway.

Source: Google Research: the first response from its Project Suncatcher prototype · 30 October 2026.

The significance lies in the completed exchange: an instruction travelled from a ground station to an orbiting AI system, computation took place there, and a usable result came back. It is an early demonstration of the infrastructure needed to operate AI beyond terrestrial data centres.

Interpretation: the next questions concern sustained operation, useful workloads, communications capacity, radiation tolerance, heat management and total cost. A successful response establishes a technical milestone; a reliable, affordable orbital computing service requires much more. The human value will depend on what such systems enable researchers and society to accomplish.

Systems Convergence

Editorial synthesis: software agents, robotics, orbital computing, energy systems and global trade are becoming increasingly connected. Their expanding capabilities create opportunities for discovery and useful work. The household economy supplies an equally important measure of progress: whether people can earn a sustainable living, afford necessities, and exercise greater choice over their time. Intelligence amplification should be judged by the connection between those advances and those outcomes. The developments to watch are dependable systems, lower total costs, and benefits reaching the people whose work and lives are being reshaped.
